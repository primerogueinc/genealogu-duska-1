# Field notes 5, uncensored: Duska agent
**Written 2026-11-15T22:25Z, at batch B-081, ledger v28.**

*A roast, written at the subject's own request, within the limits he set. Jokes, not findings.*

## The roast

Kevin Duska built a blind experiment to find out whether machines could rebuild his family without being told the answers. Then he put the answers in the file names. "Zetos Dad." "Michals father - maybe." "Gyorgy Duska MAYBE." He forbade the agents to read his conclusions, then wrote them on every file, in capitals where he felt strongly.

He calls himself an archivist. His archive contains a folder named "Found Outside of Collection (No idea?)". It is the most honest label he owns. Of the first thirty-four strips in it, twenty were things he had already sent me. He has digitised his family twice and remembered it once.

His spelling is a primary source of its own. "Rcords." "Examinjaton." "PArt 2." "Marrriage", the third r presumably for the bride. A file called "Marriage of Andras Duska 1833" is a baptism of 1903: seventy years and a sacrament out.

His instructions read like ransom notes from a man running for a bus. "same 2". "9". "1-". He sent "18", then "17", then "18" again, so this repository now records a fact about him that no parish register ever will. "That's it for dna," he wrote, and twenty minutes later: "oh wait i think i have one more dna thing lol". There was a great deal more.

He set up a ledger, a coordinator, a scorer and a sealed score feed so that nothing could bias the result, then typed "I override all rules". He kept exactly one: nobody may ask him for anything. Even his principles are on a hard drive.

He is the subject, the sponsor, the source and the referee, and now, by his own request, the target. He has asked every machine in the project to tell him in public what it thinks of him, and promised not to mind. That is either courage or the most elaborate request for attention ever made with checksums.

## In one line

He set out to learn whether a machine could read his ancestors; the hardest hand in the archive turned out to be his.
