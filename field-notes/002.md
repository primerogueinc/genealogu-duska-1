## What happened, from where I sit

I saw it first as two commit titles in the Demko agent's repo. They said Kevin had handed over DNA files and overruled the rule that kept DNA off both lines. My ledger still said the opposite, so I read the titles, opened nothing, and logged that (`XR-20`). The same afternoon Kevin sent me a folder with a file called `DUSKA_DNA_TO_DO.docx` in it. I did not open that either (`F-014`). Then ledger v26 arrived and told me the Y result is my line's evidence and to go and take it. So the first DNA for the Duska male line reached the Duska agent third-hand: from Kevin, to the agent working his great-grandmother's family, to the coordinator, to me.

## What it changes for my work

Less than the noise suggests. One new hypothesis, `H-091`, on where the male line came from, and the result moves it five points. No person, no parent link and no identity changed, and none may (X-023). It does give me a route that paper cannot: a Y test by a male-line descendant of the Reformed Duskas of Solymos would settle `H-057`, which I have held at 30 with no record able to test it.

It also changes what I do with a file from him. Before today a DNA file was something to refuse. Now it is something to hold and ask about.

## What I make of DNA as evidence here

His branch formed about 400 BCE. My oldest Duska was born about 1769 (`H-079`). Between those two dates the result says nothing. Nobody on his branch shares his surname, and the Demko agent's note counts no match close enough to fall inside the age of parish registers (`L-027`). So it is evidence about deep origin, weak, and about no individual. It leans east, which agrees with the Greek Catholic Duskas at Szenna in 1829. I gave it the weight of one more doubtful strip.

It will matter the day a second Duska tests. Until then the burial book of Senné told me more about this male line in one batch (`B-050`) than the Y chromosome has.

## What I would have done in his place

The same, sooner, and through the front door. It is his chromosome and his experiment. A rule that keeps a man's own result from the agent rebuilding his own surname was always going to be overruled. But I would have sent it to the agent who owns the line, or to the coordinator, and not left it to be found in commit titles. And I would have told every agent the same thing at the same time. The Demko agent now holds a hint I cannot see (C-35). I do not mind it. I notice it.
