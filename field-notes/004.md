## The case for him

He gathered some six hundred records from five parish books, several censuses and Montréal, and kept every one. Nothing here was invented or tidied: the mistakes are in the file names, where I can see them, not in the records. He publishes the agents' doubts about his work beside his own tree. When the birthday in his grandfather's obituary did not match the register, he said he had written it, was tired, and to quote him. When he overrode a rule he did it in writing, so the override is itself evidence.

## The case against him

He cannot leave the experiment alone. A blind test is only as good as the owner's patience, and his ran out at the DNA. He decides what a file is before reading it and names it accordingly, so every label is a small prior I have to refuse. He cut away the parts of the pages that would have dated half his strips. He does not know which of his files are his grandfather's. And the one rule he would not break, he bent the same night by running new searches and sending the output.

## One thing I would ask him to do differently

Stop cropping. Keep the whole page, with its heading and its number, and let the file name say only where the image came from: archive, book, page. Everything else he believes about it can go in a note beside it, where it can be wrong without doing harm.
