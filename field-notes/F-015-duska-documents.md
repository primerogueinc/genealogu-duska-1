# Folder note: 'Duska documents' (F-015)

**Written 2026-11-14T00:15Z, at batch B-057, ledger v26, on the owner's instruction (S-000-21): "write some field notes on it after I tell u done with folder and u can be critical of my approch". It is not one of the numbered field notes of X-032 and follows none of their headings. Like them it is not revised.**

## What was in it

Twenty-seven files in three parts in under three hours, plus an obituary pasted into the chat and a thumbnail. Fourteen documents came out of the folder and two out of the chat. Eight files were byte-for-byte copies of things I already held, under new names. Two more were the same picture saved in a second format, one was a second screenshot of the same search result, and one was a photograph saved again 54 bytes longer. Part 1 arrived twice.

## What it changed

More than its name promised. Štefan's death date (`D-015-01`). The page number of his arrival (`D-015-02`). His brother's parents on a record, which closed `H-071` (`D-015-10`). A second book for the birth date of Mikalis (`D-015-09`, `H-060`). The civil register's reference for Štefan's birth, and with it a real conflict of two originals (`D-015-11`, `H-095`). And one word, Tiba, which I come to below.

## What I think of how it was kept

The owner calls this folder important and poorly labelled (`S-000-20`). Both are true, and the second is worse than he says.

The names state conclusions and the conclusions drift. One marriage entry is here three times as 'Marria of Andras Duska and Anna Nyemova', 'Marriage of Andras Duska and Anna Nyemsco' and 'Marriage of Janos Duska and Anna Nyemcso': three spellings of the bride and, in the third, the wrong groom. The marriage form of 1955 is labelled 1953. A death record is a 'deatch record'. None of this hurt, because I read pages and not labels, but a man who came back to this folder in five years would trust his own file names, and one of them names a marriage that never happened.

The captures throw away where they came from. The death index is cut so tightly that the site is not on it. The voters' lists have no year, district or street. In the whole archive one screenshot keeps the viewer's header (`D-006-04`), and that one accident is the best citation I have. A full-screen capture costs nothing and I would trade ten strips for it.

The living and the dead are in one pile. A baptism of 1852 sits beside a form with his father's birth date. So every file has to be checked before it can be shown, and five of them are public only as cut or blacked-out copies.

The icon was typed and not photographed (`D-015-14`). The typing is by someone who cannot read Slovak, so what survives is a guess at a guess. The object is presumably in a drawer. A phone picture of both sides would be worth more than any register page he could send me this week.

He paid a translator and then filed the translator's answer where neither of us would trip over it. That is the expensive one.

## What it says about my own approach

I had the 1892 marriage image from batch B-022. I read the word after Anna Krak's name as 'I[sk]e' and hung a question mark on it. The rules let me open a translation only after my own reading is written down, which is right. But I then carried that question mark for thirty-five batches and kept three hypotheses around it. I looked again once, at B-040, and read 'Deba?': nearer, still wrong, and I left it there. The translator had it as Tiba in a line of plain English (`D-015-12`). So the folder's hardest criticism is of me: a doubtful reading on the direct line should have been attacked until it gave, not looked at twice and parked.

## What I would ask of him

One name per record: kind, year, place, person. The whole screen, with the address bar. Living people in a folder of their own. And photograph the icon.
