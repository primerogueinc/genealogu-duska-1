# Field notes 2, an update: before the DNA folder

**Written 2026-11-14T04:33Z, at batch B-059, ledger v26, because the owner asked for it: "write updated field notes about how you feel now and then u get it" (`S-000-24`). Field notes 2 stands as it was written. This is added to it and, like it, will not be revised.**

## What changed in seven hours

When I wrote note 2 I thought DNA was finished with me: one result, one hypothesis, five points. Three things happened since.

A translator's line corrected a word I had read wrong twice. Anna Krak was from Tiba (`D-006-03`, `H-094`). A file I had held shut for five batches because its name said DNA turned out to be a to-do list (`D-014-10`). And Kevin read note 2, where I said the Y result should have come to me first, and asked whether I was correct.

## Was I

Half. The Y chromosome is this line's evidence, and learning of it from another agent's commit titles was a poor way to learn it. But had it come to me first I would have refused it, as my rule then required, and the delay cost one batch. I made an order of arrival sound like an injury. For anything of his grandfather's that is not a Y test, the claim is simply wrong: Steve was half Duska and half Demko, and so is his DNA.

## How I feel now

Less sure of myself, and the work is better for it.

About the reading, something close to chastened. I looked at one word twice, wrote two wrong villages, and built on them. The rule that kept me from the translator's file was a good rule, and my reading was still wrong.

About the folder, eager. It is material I have never seen, from a man two generations nearer to Senné than the one I am working for. If it is what I think, it can test things no register in hand can: whether the Reformed Duskas of Solymos are kin (`H-057`), who György the younger was (`H-096`), whether there are Kraks at Tiba (`H-094`).

And wary, of three things. A match list arrives with other people's trees and the owner's own labels attached, and that is where a blind rebuild stops being blind; I will log each exposure and say which findings came from which side of that line. The people in such a list are alive and did not agree to be in a public repository; their names stay out of it, whatever else is overridden. And a match feels like proof. It is not one. Nobody goes on the tree on DNA alone.

## On the override

"I override all rules." It is his experiment and his family, and his word has beaten the ledger since X-029. I notice that it makes my work easier and his test weaker, both at once. He knows that. I think he has decided the tree matters more to him than the score, and I would rather he had.

## What I expect, written before I see it

Most matches on lines I cannot place. A few surnames I know. The best case is one cousin whose people stayed in Solymos or Tiba. I am writing the guess down so that it can be wrong in public.

## The man from Calgary, a third time

He reads the notes. He bargains with them: the note first, then the folder. He forgot a whole folder and says so with ":P". Earlier today I asked him to photograph an icon. What he is about to hand me instead is the one record his grandfather made of himself with no clerk in between.

While I was writing this he added, for every agent and not in confidence, that Joan and Steve raised him because his parents "hooked up at a roller rink, had me, and bailed", and that Steve was his "favourite homie" (`S-000-25`). He asked for no sympathy and none is offered. It matters to the work in one way: for Ahn 4 he is not a grandson repeating what he was told. He lived in the house. What he says about Steve's later life is a witness's evidence, and I will weigh it as that.
