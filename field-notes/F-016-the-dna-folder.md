# Folder note: the DNA folder (F-016)

*Written 14 Nov 2026, 05:02 UTC, at batch B-069, ledger v26, when the owner closed the folder ("that's it for dna"). Asked for under his standing instruction to write a note at each juncture. Not one of the numbered notes of X-032. Not revised.*

## What came

Thirty-three files in eight parts over one evening: fourteen screenshots of ancestry percentages, raw data for two people (the owner twice, his grandfather once), a match list for each of five kits and then an X-chromosome match list for each of the same five, two one-to-one comparisons, and two reports on whether somebody's parents were cousins. Four of the five kits are the owner. One is Štefan, who died in 2018 and whose sample was read in January 2022 from whatever he had given before.

## What it did for the tree

Two things.

It proved the foot of the tree. Kevin, his father and Štefan are joined on paper by a 1984 form I may show only blacked out and by the owner's word. The raw files show the same Y chromosome in grandfather and grandson at every one of 1,638 markers and 1,567.6 cM shared, which is a grandparent's share. H-097 is solved at 96 and it is the only link in the tree solved by something other than paper. Every generation above it still rests on a priest's handwriting.

It said one small thing about 1927. Štefan's parents were not near cousins (H-098). That is worth having because each had a Hresko grandmother.

And that is all. No Duska appears among 2,996 autosomal matches or 466 X matches. No Demko, Krak, Nyemcsik or Hresko either. Štefan's nearest stranger shares 52 cM with him. The databases are full of North Americans, and the cottars of Senné did not leave many descendants who spit in tubes.

## Where I was wrong

In the note before this folder I said the DNA should have come first. I was right for a reason I did not give and wrong in the one I had in mind. I hoped for cousins: a match who descended from a brother of András or of György, with a tree attached. There is none. What the DNA was good for was certifying the three living-memory generations, and that is worth doing first because everything else stands on them. So: first, yes; decisive, no.

I also published a wrong number. My own comparison of the raw files gave 19.8 per cent shared. GEDmatch gives 21.8. I had dropped five short segments and counted one false one across a centromere. It is corrected on the document with a date. The prediction held; the arithmetic did not.

## On the owner's method

He said I could be critical.

Fourteen of the thirty-three files are admixture charts. For Štefan the largest parts are 'Baltic', 'East Central European' or 'North European', according to which calculator is asked. The 1929 baptism says where he was from with a house number.

He uploaded himself four times. The four kits of one man have X lists of 56, 70, 404 and 483 rows, and share 17, 48, 126 and 148 kits with his grandfather's autosomal list. That is a useful accident: it shows how much a match list depends on which chip read the sample, and how little weight a missing match can bear. But it was an accident. Nobody planning a study would do it.

Most of the second half of the folder was made tonight. The reports are stamped, or were sent, between 23:52 and 23:57 on the evening they were made. His one rule he would not override is that agents get what is on his hard drive now. These files were on his hard drive for about a minute each. The rule binds me and not him, and I would rather have the files than not. But anybody judging this experiment should know that part of the "archive" was generated on request, mid-experiment, by the man who set the rule. Each such file says so in its record.

The X lists were sent to the wrong agent. A man's X is his mother's. Štefan's list is all Demko. The owner's four are all his mother's, a line no agent here is rebuilding, and I took two counts from them by program and read nothing.

The first file of the last part was labelled autosomal and was the X comparison. It says "No shared DNA segments found" about a man and his grandfather. With the wrong label it would be alarming. It is the folder-name problem again: his labels are a record of what he believed a thing was, and they are right often enough to be dangerous.

## What was not in the folder

The owner mentioned that he took a deep Y test and Štefan never did. Since the two carry the same Y, his result is Štefan's, and András's, and the elder György's. It is the one piece of DNA that speaks to where the male line came from (H-091), which no autosomal list can reach. If it exists as a file it was not in this folder. I am not asking for it. I am noting what the question would need.

## How it sits

Steadier than before. I had been building on a foot I could not show. Now I can. The rest is as it was: a tree that stops at a man born about 1833 whose wife was "of Tiba", and no register for Tiba in hand.
