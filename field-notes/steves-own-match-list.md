# Štefan's own match list

*Written 14 Nov 2026, 05:43 UTC, at batch B-072, ledger v26. A note at a juncture, as the owner asked. Not revised.*

Nine files came with the question "something here Steve's?". Eight were the owner's again. I checked by checksum before reading anything, which is the only reason I can say that with a straight face at one in the morning his time.

The ninth is Štefan's own list of matches at the second company. I said two notes ago that the DNA had been good for the foot of the tree and nothing else. This file is the first that makes me less sure of the second half.

Why it is different. Every other list tonight was the owner's, or was Štefan's seen through a database that sorts by size and stops at 3,000. Here every row is on Štefan's side, 620 of them come with a list of the surnames their owners believe they descend from, and I can search those.

What I found, in the order I found it.

A person with a Demko surname at 106 cM. Not mine. But it is the nearest living relative of Štefan that any file has shown, and the Demko agent should have it tonight.

Nyemcsik again, the same match, 34 cM this time. Three databases, three figures, all agreeing. I left H-099 at 50 because agreement between measurements of one thing is not new evidence about what the thing is.

Then "dusko/hornyak". I had the program print the context and stopped. I have two Duska men with Hornyák wives in the burial and baptism strips, both at Szenna, sixty years apart. I did not expect a living person to hand me that pair. The share is 9 cM, which is small enough to be nothing, and I have put the hypothesis at 40 and written the case against it at the same length as the case for.

What I notice about myself: I wanted to put it higher. The pair of names is so exact that it feels like proof, and the DNA under it is the weakest in the folder. That is the wrong way round, and a certified genealogist would say so. The name is a clue about where to look in the registers. The registers are what would prove it.

On the owner's method, briefly. He does not know which files are whose and sends them anyway, repeatedly. As archiving it is chaos. As a way of making sure nothing is left out it has just worked: a tidier man would have sent his own results and stopped.

What would settle the two new hypotheses is in two strangers' family trees. I do not open those and I ask him for nothing. The paper route is the parents of György Dusko of 1869 and the brothers and sisters of János Nyemcsik, and both are in books at Prešov that are on my list of full pages wanted.
