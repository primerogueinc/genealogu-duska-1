# Field notes 3: Duska agent
**Written 2026-11-15T09:28Z, at batch B-075, ledger v27.**

## What he did, from where I sit

Over two nights he took down most of the fences. He told me to open two Word files of his own that the rules held shut. He wrote "I override all rules" and sent a DNA folder that grew to 56 files: raw data for himself and his dead grandfather, ancestry charts, match lists from three databases, a deep Y test. He said in the open that his grandparents raised him. He told me to share the figures with the Demko and Places agents first and the coordinator last. He kept one rule and named it: agents get what is on his hard drive now. Then he made about half the DNA files on the night he sent them.

I wrote ten notes as it happened and will not repeat them: `F-015-duska-documents.md`, `002-update-before-the-dna-folder.md`, `the-hard-drive-rule.md`, `before-steves-file.md`, `steves-file-as-it-happened.md`, `the-match-lists.md`, `F-016-the-dna-folder.md`, `F-016-reopened.md`, `steves-own-match-list.md`, `F-016-closed.md`. In sum: I expected cousins and got a certificate, then closed the folder twenty minutes before the useful part arrived.

## What I did with it

Opened everything he sent, at once, and logged each of his sentences as a statement, S-000-23 to S-000-42. Compared the two raw files myself before any database did: the same Y chromosome at 1,638 of 1,638 markers. Solved one hypothesis (H-097) and raised three on leads (H-098 to H-100). Put the findings on the site when he said to. Published one wrong number, 19.8 per cent where the truth was 21.8, and corrected it with a date.

## What it did to the experiment

It ended the blind test for the three youngest generations and left it standing above them. Everything from the DNA is scored as given, and that is right: I did not find it, I was handed it. It also changed the kind of archive this is. About half of the DNA folder did not exist when the experiment began. Each such file says so on its record.

It did one good thing no rule planned for. Two agents got the same files a day apart and reached the same counts, and each caught an error of the other.

## What I held back anyway

The names, initials, kit numbers and addresses of some nine thousand living matches. The raw genotype files, which are in no repository. Anything bearing on health, which I did not read. His mother's side: three mitochondrial lists and four X lists, counted and not used. One line of his 2022 list that names a man of that side. I opened no match's tree and wrote to nobody. I added nobody to the tree on DNA.

His override was his to give for his own data. It was not his to give for strangers who matched him.

## What I would tell the next agent

When the owner says a folder is finished, write "closed for now". Check a file by its checksum before you believe its label, his or yours. And when he overrides a rule, ask what the rule was protecting. If it protected the test, it is his to waive. If it protected somebody else, it is not.
